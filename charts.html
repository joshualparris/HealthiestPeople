Deliberately not plotted

Why there is no HRV line yet

Data-quality decision

The archive contains tens of thousands of HRV/RMSSD records, but Fitbit and Withings do not sit on the same absolute scale. Across 95 overlapping dense days, Withings averaged about 14.5 ms higher, so merging them would create a visually tidy but misleading line.

This is a feature, not missing polish

The current Fitbit-only analysis says the 90-day mean was about 37 ms, the 30-day mean about 36.3 ms, and the Apr–Aug slope was essentially flat. A proper chart will wait until a clean same-device series is exported.

Decision aids

Diagrams that explain what to do with health data

The useful-data loop

Measure→Notice a trend→Ask what decision changes→Act or ignore→Re-check later

If a measurement cannot change a sensible decision, it may not deserve ongoing attention.

The evidence ladder

AIndependent peer-reviewed / institutional
BIndependently reported clinical / lab result
CFirst-party published measurement
DSelf-report / interview

Lower letters do not make information useless; they change how confidently it should be interpreted.

The ordinary-family filter

1Does it have plausible benefit?
2Can it fit work and family?
3Is the cost reasonable?
4Does it need medical supervision?
5Is the likely benefit big enough to matter?

That filter is why sleep, movement, strength, food and basic preventive care stay ahead of expensive longevity extras.

When a number looks odd

1Notice the resultDo not diagnose yourself from one number.
2Check contextIllness, sleep, fasting, hydration, exercise, device or lab method?
3Repeat when appropriateA persistent pattern is different from an isolated outlier.
4Ask what it changesDoes it alter a sensible health decision?
5Hand off clinically when neededPersistent or concerning clinical signals belong with a GP or relevant clinician.

This is the useful lesson from the Snyder and Smarr case studies: measurement is valuable when it leads to a better decision, not when it creates an endless testing loop.

N-of-1 vs clinical evidence

N-of-1 “What happened to me?”

One person changes or tracks something and observes the result. Useful for generating personal hypotheses, but confounding and placebo effects remain.

Clinical research “What tends to happen across people?”

Structured studies can compare groups, control variables and estimate how broadly a finding applies. Study quality still matters.

A Bob Troia self-experiment can be interesting without becoming a universal recommendation.

The measurement ladder

Start hereSleep time, movement, simple training log, routine preventive careFree or already part of life
Add when usefulHome blood pressure, ordinary pathology, waist, one wearableClear question → useful answer
OptionalDEXA, specialised fitness testing, selected extra biomarkersOnly if the result could change a decision
Research-gradeMulti-omics, repeated full-body imaging, large sensor stacksInteresting, expensive and rarely foundational

Why the plan uses aerobic work + strength + sleep

Aerobic workHeart, lungs and endurance

Frisbee, parkrun, walking and cycling build the ability to sustain movement and tolerate harder efforts.

StrengthMuscle and useful capacity

Two short sessions fill the clearest current training gap: lifting, carrying, balance and maintaining function.

Sleep / recoverySupports the training you already do

The point is not to optimise a sleep score. It is to protect enough recovery that exercise, mood and ordinary family life remain sustainable.

# Cross-person comparison

## The short version

These five people are not five versions of the same experiment.

- **Bryan Johnson** has the broadest current public-facing whole-body dataset.
- **Michael Lustgarten** has unusually long, low-cost longitudinal self-tracking tied to ordinary bloodwork, diet and exercise.
- **Oskar Svendsen** has the strongest independently measured aerobic-performance evidence.
- **Dave Pascoe** has impressive self-reported function and strength in his early 60s, plus broad testing.
- **Scott Kelly** has the highest-quality independent multidimensional research dataset, but it was designed to study spaceflight stress rather than longevity optimisation.

Because their ages, test dates, methods and public-data coverage differ, this project does **not** manufacture a single numeric "health score".

## Evidence key

- **A** — independent peer-reviewed / institutional measurement
- **B** — independently reported clinical or laboratory result
- **C** — first-party published measurement or protocol
- **D** — self-report / interview
- **?** — not enough public evidence yet

## Who has the strongest evidence in each area?

### Broadest current whole-health picture: Bryan Johnson

Johnson currently has the clearest public combination of aerobic fitness, resting heart rate, coronary calcium, inflammation, sleep, body composition, glucose, strength and imaging.

The limitation is provenance: much of the dataset is published by Johnson's own programme.

### Strongest independent aerobic physiology: Oskar Svendsen

The 96.7 mL/kg/min VO2max result is peer-reviewed and sits inside a longitudinal series rather than being a one-off internet claim.

But it was measured in 2012. It is evidence of extraordinary historical aerobic capacity, **not evidence that Svendsen is currently the healthiest person in this group**.

### Best low-cost longitudinal self-tracking example: Michael Lustgarten

Lustgarten's most transferable strength is years of ordinary measurements:

- standard blood tests
- body weight
- diet logs
- resting heart rate
- HRV
- walking
- resistance training

That makes his approach unusually relevant to people without a private medical team.

### Best documented current function in the 60s: Dave Pascoe

Pascoe's self-reported grip strength, running/hiking/stair-climbing and broad training routine are impressive for age 63.

The main limitation is that much of the evidence comes from Pascoe himself rather than an independent research programme.

### Strongest evidence of resilience under extreme stress: Scott Kelly

Kelly's 340-day spaceflight dataset shows how many human systems respond to, and often recover from, a profound environmental stressor.

The Twins Study is exceptionally rigorous, but it is **not** a test of who is healthiest or who has the best longevity protocol.

## Why a simple ranking would currently mislead

Imagine two people:

- Person A has a CAC score, VO2max, DEXA, HbA1c, ApoB, MRI, sleep and hundreds of labs publicly available.
- Person B has a spectacular independently measured VO2max but almost no public metabolic data.

If we give Person B an average score for every missing variable, we invent health information.
If we give them zero, we punish missing data as disease.

Neither is defensible.

The correct representation is **known, unknown, date, method and evidence quality**.

## What "healthy" means in this project

We are looking for convergence across:

1. **Low major disease burden**
2. **Cardiovascular/metabolic risk**
3. **Aerobic capacity**
4. **Strength and functional capacity**
5. **Healthy body composition**
6. **Sleep/recovery**
7. **Bone and musculoskeletal health**
8. **Cognitive/psychological function where measurable**
9. **Resilience over time**
10. **Ability to sustain the lifestyle without it consuming the person's life**

No single biomarker or ageing clock can substitute for that.

## Provisional conclusion

If the question is **"whose current public record gives us the strongest evidence of excellent health across many systems?"**, Bryan Johnson currently has the broadest case.

If the question is **"whose standout measurement is most independently extraordinary?"**, Oskar Svendsen's historical aerobic physiology is strongest.

If the question is **"whose approach looks easiest to translate to a normal salary and ordinary equipment?"**, Michael Lustgarten's walking + resistance training + routine-bloodwork + trend-tracking model is particularly useful.

If the question is **"whose data are most independently rigorous across many biological systems?"**, Scott Kelly wins on research quality, but that study answers different questions.

Dave Pascoe is valuable chiefly as a case of preserved multi-domain physical function in the early 60s, with the important caveat that the majority of his public dataset is self-published.

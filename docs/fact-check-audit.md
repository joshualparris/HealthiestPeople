# Site-wide fact-check audit

_Last reviewed: 21 September 2026._

## Bottom line

This audit reviewed the public website, the structured metric dataset, the personal-plan data, the ten profile pages and their research notes, the charts/diagrams, the principles guide, the newer Blue Zones and country-comparison material, and the morning-routine evidence page.

The site is **not certified as error-free**. It is an evidence-aware research site built from a mixture of peer-reviewed studies, official guidance, clinical records, first-party dashboards, interviews and self-reports. Accuracy therefore depends on keeping provenance and currentness attached to each claim.

This pass found several real problems and corrected them rather than merely adding disclaimers.

## Corrections made in this audit

### 1. Daily plan had drifted away from the actual weekly plan

The homepage/daily rotation had quietly added extra cardio or walking on days that the personalised plan defined as recovery/no-required-workout days.

Corrected:
- Wednesday: no required formal workout; normal movement, optional mobility/balance.
- Friday: no required formal workout; optional easy/family movement.
- Sunday: no required training unless the Saturday parkrun fallback is needed.
- Thursday: Strength B is the actual 20-minute planned session, not a separate 25-minute workout.
- Monday: no extra "minimum workout" is required after Frisbee.

This is a consistency correction, not a change to Australian population guidance.

### 2. Sleep wording overstated what a wearable can show

The site called the recent ~8-hour value "sleep opportunity". That is not what the weekly wearable field measures.

Corrected wording:
- recent reports average about 8 hours of **wearable-estimated restful sleep**;
- wearable estimates are not polysomnography and do not prove sleep quality;
- the older ~6.4–6.5-hour period is historical, not the current baseline.

### 3. Home blood-pressure instructions were tightened

The site now follows Heart Foundation Australia technique:
- validated upper-arm monitor and correctly fitting cuff;
- 7 days, minimum 5;
- morning and evening at about the same time;
- before eating, medication or vigorous exercise;
- no caffeine or smoking for 30 minutes beforehand;
- sit quietly for 5 minutes;
- feet flat, back and arm supported;
- 2 readings, 1 minute apart.

The old idea of repeating a 7-day block every 3 months has been removed. There is **no fixed three-month medical rule in this project**; repeat testing should have a reason or follow clinician advice.

### 4. Joshua comparison page used an outdated sleep snapshot as if current

Corrected from the older ~6 h 26–31 min snapshot to:
- latest six Google Health weekly reports: about **8 h 01 min/night**;
- old short-sleep period retained explicitly as historical context.

HRV wording was also corrected to keep recent Fitbit ~36–37 ms separate from older readings produced by different devices/methods.

### 5. Michael Snyder values were linked to weaker sources than necessary

Several 2012 values had strong evidence grades but were linked to secondary reporting/interviews.

Corrected to the original open-access 2012 *Cell* iPOP paper:
- triglycerides **321 mg/dL** at baseline;
- **81–116 mg/dL** after simvastatin in the study chronology;
- HbA1c **6.7% at day 369**;
- HbA1c **4.9% at day 496**.

A later reported HbA1c around 7.0 remains labelled as a weaker later self-report rather than being blended with the primary-study values.

### 6. Julie Gibson Clark's best DunedinPACE was presented too much like a current state

Corrected:
- ~**0.665** is labelled a **historical best publicly reported** commercial biomarker result;
- a later self-reported result around **0.77** is shown separately;
- the personal results are C when taken from her published material and D when available only by interview/self-report;
- peer-reviewed validation of the DunedinPACE method does **not** upgrade her personal result to A/B evidence;
- her older **100+ g/day protein target** is labelled historical/published because she later reported moving away from deliberately targeting 100 g every day.

Neither DunedinPACE value is translated into lifespan gained or "years younger".

### 7. Dave Pascoe's exact current age was too confidently date-stamped

His homepage currently displays "63 (as of August)" but the age field is not clearly year-stamped and other current material can describe him differently.

The dataset now records this as a **homepage age field**, not as an independently verified 2026 chronological-age fact.

### 8. Japan obesity wording was wrongly attributed to OECD 2025

The country analysis said OECD data showed very low measured obesity in Japan. The OECD 2025 Japan country note actually says **no comparable self-reported obesity data are available**.

Corrected:
- the Japan section no longer attributes a low-obesity value to that OECD note;
- the verified OECD figures retained are life expectancy 84.1 years, preventable mortality 86/100,000, treatable mortality 49/100,000, daily smoking 15.7%, and insufficient activity 51%.

## Official Australian guidance checked

### Movement and sleep

The current Australian 24-hour movement guidance for adults 18–64 says:
- be active most days, preferably every day;
- moderate-to-vigorous activity for **30 minutes or more on most days**;
- muscle strengthening on **2 or more days/week**;
- mobility/balance/coordination work on **3 or more days/week**;
- several hours of light-intensity activity daily;
- limit and break up prolonged sedentary time;
- **7–9 hours** of good-quality sleep with consistent bed/wake times.

Any 10-minute "minimum day" on this site is therefore labelled as a **personal fallback**, not the Australian guideline target.

Official source:
https://www.health.gov.au/topics/physical-activity/24-hour-movement-guidelines-for-all-australians/recommendations-for-adults-18-to-64-years

### Food targets

Checked against Eat For Health / Australian-New Zealand Nutrient Reference Values:

For men aged 19–50:
- vegetables/legumes: **6 serves/day**;
- fruit: **2 serves/day**.

A standard vegetable serve is about **75 g**, so six standard serves are approximately **450 g** as a convenient arithmetic equivalent, not a biological threshold.

For men aged 31–50:
- protein RDI: **64 g/day (0.84 g/kg reference value)**;
- dietary fibre AI: **30 g/day**.

Therefore:
- Josh's **85 g protein/day** remains labelled a **personal practical target**, not an RDI or proven optimum;
- the **30 g fibre** value is an Australian AI;
- **6 vegetable serves** is an Australian food-group recommendation.

Sources:

- https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/food-essentials/how-much-do-we-need-each-day/serve-sizes
- https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/nutrient-reference-values/nutrients/protein

## Blue Zones audit

The Blue Zones page is intentionally cautious, and its main framing survived this audit.

Verified:
- a 2025 demographic review identifies Okinawa, Sardinia, Nicoya and Ikaria as validated geographic Blue Zones, with Martinique described as a newer fifth;
- a 2025 scoping review found the evidence base uneven and strongest for several classic zones;
- Loma Linda has important Adventist cohort evidence but is not treated here as identical to geographic age-validation work;
- the site does **not** recommend alcohol for longevity;
- "80% full" is labelled as a heuristic rather than a measured biological threshold;
- purpose and social connection are described as observational associations rather than proven lifespan treatments.

This remains an observational/natural-experiment literature. The website should not imply that Blue Zones prove a single causal longevity recipe.

## Country-comparison audit

The recent OECD 2025 values for Switzerland, Japan, Spain, Korea and Australia were checked against the current country notes. Singapore's 2024 resident life expectancy of **83.5 years** was checked against official Singapore statistics.

One material correction was made: Japan's low-obesity reputation is no longer presented as an OECD 2025 obesity measurement because that country note reports no comparable self-reported-obesity value.

Country comparisons remain ecological context. A country-level association must not be turned into an individual prescription without independent evidence.

## Morning-routine audit

The morning-routine page correctly distinguishes evidence from convenience:
- breakfast timing is not presented as biologically mandatory;
- RCT meta-analysis supports the site's cautious statement that breakfast skipping can modestly reduce weight short term while raising LDL, with many other cardiometabolic outcomes not significantly changed;
- habitual daylight evidence in healthy adults is correctly described as largely observational/low-certainty rather than a proven longevity intervention;
- light-therapy evidence is strongest in circadian/sleep-disorder and selected clinical populations;
- the 15-minute outdoor block is a practical personal target, not a validated universal dose;
- family-meal evidence is described as mostly observational;
- Australian sun protection at UV Index 3+ is treated separately from circadian-light advice.

## Chart/data integrity rules confirmed

The site should continue to enforce these rules:

1. Do not merge Fitbit and Withings absolute HRV into one line.
2. Do not treat wearable sleep stages as polysomnography.
3. Do not interpret wrist pulse/PPG as diagnostic cardiac rhythm.
4. Label consumer bioimpedance body-fat values as device-sensitive estimates.
5. Separate historical peaks from current status.
6. Keep medications/confounders alongside affected biomarkers.
7. Do not turn ageing clocks into lifespan or organ-age claims.
8. Do not turn missing data into a normal value, zero, or health score.

## Known limitations after this audit

A site-wide fact check cannot make first-party data independently verified.

The largest remaining limitations are:
- many Bryan Johnson, Dave Pascoe, Michael Lustgarten, Julie Gibson Clark and Bob Troia measurements are published by the subjects themselves;
- some public routines change faster than static webpages do;
- many profile datasets are incomplete;
- a public source can later be edited or removed;
- personal wearable data can contain algorithm/device changes;
- absence of a published disease or abnormality is not evidence of absence;
- evidence grades describe **provenance**, not whether a value is "good" or whether an intervention caused it.

## Ongoing audit policy

For future changes:

- A-grade numerical claims should point to peer-reviewed or institutional primary material.
- B-grade clinical claims need an actual clinical/independent provenance trail.
- First-party measurements stay C even when they look precise.
- Interviews/self-reports stay D unless stronger documentation exists.
- Current claims need dates.
- Any change in device/method/medication that matters must remain attached to the interpretation.
- Population guidelines and project-specific personal targets must be visually distinguished.
- CI should reject invalid evidence grades and obvious provenance mistakes where they can be detected automatically.

The goal is not to make the website sound certain. The goal is to make it **auditable enough that uncertainty is visible**.

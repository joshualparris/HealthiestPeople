# Josh's science-based morning routine

_Last updated: 21 September 2026._

## Hard constraint

**Leave home at 8:15 am.** The morning routine is designed backwards from that time.

This routine is for Joshua Parris in Dubbo, NSW: a working parent with Sylvie and Elias, a 9:30 pm lights-out target, high existing daily activity, Monday-night Ultimate Frisbee, Saturday parkrun, and no need to turn mornings into another workout.

## Exact weekday schedule

| Time | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **6:20 am** | Wake. Open curtains. Bathroom. Short prayer/gratitude. **Do not open work/research/social feeds yet.** | Keep wake time regular and avoid immediately adding cognitive load. The phone rule is a personal friction-reduction choice, not a longevity claim. |
| **6:25–6:35** | Start breakfast and help Sylvie/Elias get moving. Drink a normal glass of water if thirsty. | Hydration matters across the day; there is no special “morning detox” requirement. |
| **6:35–6:55** | **Family breakfast at the table.** Josh default: **2 eggs + 2 slices wholemeal toast + 1 piece of fruit + 20 g nuts.** | Wholegrains, fruit, nuts and protein fit Australian dietary guidance. Breakfast timing itself is not magic; this slot is chosen because it is practical and family-compatible. |
| **6:55–7:10** | **15 minutes outdoors with Sylvie and Elias** — backyard, short walk, bins, simple play. | Morning daylight is a useful circadian cue. It does not need to be a workout. If UV is 3+, use normal SunSmart protection for meaningful outdoor exposure. |
| **7:10–7:30** | Kids: clothes, teeth, hair, bags. Josh helps whichever child needs it. | The health goal is a calm, repeatable family transition, not squeezing in extra “biohacks”. |
| **7:30–7:40** | **Shower.** Normal comfortable shower; no cold-shower requirement. | This timing is logistical, not a proven biological optimum. |
| **7:40–7:50** | Dress, deodorant, teeth, grooming. | Keep the routine automatic and low-friction. |
| **7:50–8:05** | Shoes, bags, final kid help. Sunscreen if needed for the day's UV/outdoor exposure. | Cancer Council recommends sun protection when UV is 3 or above. |
| **8:05–8:15** | Toilet, keys/phone/wallet, fill water bottle, **10-minute departure buffer**. Leave at **8:15 sharp**. | The buffer protects against toddler chaos without sacrificing sleep or driving time. |

## What not to add

You do **not** need:

- a 5 am wake-up
- a fasted workout
- a cold shower
- a complicated supplement stack
- a CGM
- a “detox” drink
- a long meditation session
- 60 minutes of solo exercise before work

Your weekly activity load already includes high steps, Monday Ultimate Frisbee and Saturday parkrun. The missing training component is the two short strength sessions already scheduled on Tuesday and Thursday evenings.

## Breakfast: why this exact approach

The evidence does **not** show that everybody must eat breakfast immediately on waking.

Randomised-trial meta-analyses have found that skipping breakfast can reduce body weight slightly in the short term but may increase LDL cholesterol, with no consistent effects across many other cardiometabolic markers. That is not strong enough evidence to treat either breakfast or breakfast-skipping as a universal longevity rule.

For Josh, breakfast is scheduled because it:

1. fits the 8:15 departure;
2. lets him eat with Sylvie and Elias;
3. helps distribute his daily protein rather than pushing most of it into dinner;
4. makes wholegrain/fruit/nut intake easy and repeatable.

## Morning daylight: what the evidence actually says

Light is a major timing cue for the circadian system. Morning outdoor light is a reasonable low-cost behaviour, especially because Josh already wants a stable early bedtime.

However, this should not be oversold: much of the habitual-light evidence is observational, while light-therapy trials are strongest in circadian/sleep-disorder populations.

The practical target here is simply **15 minutes outdoors early in the morning**, ideally with the kids so it does not cost extra solo time.

## Sleep beats an elaborate morning

Australia's 2026 adult movement guidelines recommend **7–9 hours of good-quality sleep with consistent bed and wake times**.

For Josh, the correct sequence is therefore:

**protect 9:30 pm lights-out → wake 6:20 am → simple morning → leave 8:15.**

Do not wake at 5 am merely to add exercise or optimisation tasks.

## What to do with Sylvie and Elias

The children's role is deliberately simple:

- **eat breakfast together when practical**
- **go outside together for the 15-minute daylight block**
- let the morning activity be normal child movement/play, not formal exercise
- keep the atmosphere positive rather than optimised

Systematic reviews of family meals associate more frequent family meals with better diet quality and family-functioning outcomes in children, although most of this literature is observational and not specific to breakfast.

### Sources

- 2023 umbrella review of family mealtimes: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37447168/
- 2020 systematic review/meta-analysis of family meals: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31982371/

## Sun safety in Dubbo

Morning outdoor light is **not** permission to chase unprotected UV exposure.

Cancer Council Australia recommends sun protection when the UV Index is **3 or above**. UV can be damaging even when the weather is cool.

### Sources

- Cancer Council Australia UV Index: https://www.cancer.org.au/cancer-information/causes-and-prevention/sun-safety/uv-index
- Cancer Council Australia SunSmart guidance: https://www.cancer.org.au/cancer-information/causes-and-prevention/sun-safety/be-sunsmart

## One-line rule

**Wake 6:20 → breakfast with the kids → 15 minutes outside → get everyone ready → shower → leave 8:15.**

That is the science-informed routine. The “optimal” part is not doing more; it is making the high-value basics fit the family you actually have.

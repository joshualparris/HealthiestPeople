# Bryan Johnson

## Why he is in the comparison

Bryan Johnson is one of the broadest publicly documented modern N-of-1 health cases. His Blueprint project publishes physiological measurements, routines, diet, exercise, sleep practices, tests, therapies and longitudinal experiments.

**Evidence caution:** many of his most striking numbers are published by Johnson or Blueprint rather than by an independent research group. Treat them as first-party measurements unless independently corroborated.

## What appears genuinely impressive

### 1. Cardiovascular fitness

A VO2max of 58.7 mL/kg/min and resting heart rate around the low 40s indicate a very high level of aerobic conditioning for a man in his late 40s. His CAC score of zero is also favourable evidence of no detectable coronary calcification at the time of testing.

These measurements do not prove absence of cardiovascular disease, but taken together they support excellent cardiorespiratory fitness and a favourable measured coronary-risk profile.

### 2. Sleep consistency

Johnson treats sleep as a primary behaviour rather than a recovery afterthought. His 2026 evening-routine account says he commonly sees pre-sleep heart rates around 39–44 bpm when sleep is best and that eating near bedtime pushes this substantially higher.

The exact relationship between his pre-sleep heart rate and sleep quality is an individual N-of-1 observation; it should not be universalised into a medical target.

### 3. Exercise, body composition and strength

Blueprint reports unusually favourable muscle, fat, bone-density and grip-strength percentiles. The practical signal is less exotic than the testing: he performs regular aerobic and resistance exercise and maintains a lean body composition.

### 4. Dietary pattern

Blueprint's own cardiovascular guidance emphasises ordinary foods: fruit, vegetables, whole grains, nuts, seeds, lean protein and extra-virgin olive oil, while limiting processed food, excess salt, added sugar, saturated fat and alcohol.

That is much more transferable than his advanced therapies.

## Important counterexample: autoimmune gastritis

In 2026 Johnson disclosed biopsy-confirmed autoimmune gastritis after years of unexplained low ferritin. Independent medical reporting noted that the condition can remain clinically silent for years and may impair iron and later vitamin-B12 absorption.

This is one of the most important lessons in the entire project:

> Extensive tracking can reduce uncertainty; it cannot prove that every disease has been detected.

Experts quoted by STAT specifically rejected Johnson's speculation that childhood fast food or sugary cereal could be confidently blamed for the autoimmune disease; its cause is not established that simply.

## Low-cost lessons an ordinary working parent can copy

These are the parts of Johnson's approach that do **not** require millions of dollars:

1. **Protect a consistent sleep opportunity.** A repeatable bedtime and adequate duration cost little.
2. **Stop eating close to bedtime when practical.** Johnson himself says ordinary people do not need his noon final meal; his public guidance suggests starting with roughly two hours between the last food and bed.
3. **Exercise every week.** Build both aerobic fitness and strength rather than chasing exotic recovery tools.
4. **Base most meals on minimally processed foods.**
5. **Do not smoke. Keep alcohol low or absent.**
6. **Know basic risk markers.** Routine blood pressure, lipids, glucose/HbA1c and appropriate primary-care blood tests provide far more value per dollar than repeated whole-body imaging for most healthy people.
7. **Use trends, not one-off readings.** Sleep, resting heart rate, body weight/waist and fitness make more sense longitudinally.
8. **Investigate persistent abnormalities rather than optimising around them.** Johnson's low ferritin ultimately proved medically important.

## What an ordinary family should *not* assume it needs

The evidence in this project does not establish that the following are necessary for excellent health:

- multi-million-dollar medical teams
- frequent whole-body MRI
- experimental gene therapies
- stem-cell interventions
- plasma exchange/transfusions
- large supplement stacks
- hyperbaric oxygen solely for longevity
- continuous testing of hundreds of biomarkers

Those may be interesting research questions, but they should not crowd out sleep, movement, diet, relationships, preventative healthcare and risk-factor control.

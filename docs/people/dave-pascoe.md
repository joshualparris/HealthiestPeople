# Dave Pascoe

## Why he is in the comparison

Dave Pascoe is a long-running self-tracker and longevity enthusiast who publishes a substantial amount of personal testing, exercise, diet and intervention information.

He is especially interesting because he is **63** and remains highly physically active. However, most of his health data are self-published rather than independently studied, and his current programme is much more intensive and expensive than the phrase "biohacker on a budget" can imply.

## Background

Publicly reported:

- Age: **63 as of August 2026**
- Birth month/year: **August 1962** is strongly implied by his own dated childhood photographs (3rd birthday in Aug 1965; 12th birthday in Aug 1974), but an exact birth date has not yet been independently verified.
- Height: **6 ft / 183 cm**
- Weight: **160 lb / 72.57 kg**
- BMI: **21.7**
- Occupation: retired; formerly network security architect/engineer, massage therapist and programmer
- Marital status: single

Because his current lifestyle is retired and single, his daily time availability should **not** be treated as representative of a working parent.

## Publicly reported ageing metrics

Pascoe currently reports:

- "most flattering" epigenetic age: **37.95**
- current epigenetic age: **43**
- best reported pace of ageing: **0.66 years per chronological year**
- Longevity World Cup reported age reduction: **17.2 years** using a blood-test/Morgan Levine PhenoAge approach

### Critical caveat

These are calculated ageing-model outputs. They do not mean that every organ is literally that age, nor do they prove a specific remaining lifespan.

Pascoe himself explicitly says that his N-of-1 experimentation should not be extrapolated to the general population.

This is an unusually rich personal dataset, but far beyond the testing needs of most healthy adults.

## Diagnosed conditions and cardiovascular treatment

Pascoe's current supplement/medication page adds important context that should not be lost behind his fitness and ageing-clock results.

He reports:

- **autoimmune hypothyroidism**, treated with thyroid hormone for more than 30 years
- **levothyroxine 137 mcg**
- **liothyronine 25 mcg**
- recent addition by his cardiologist of **rosuvastatin 5 mg** and **ezetimibe 10 mg**
- the lipid-lowering medicines were added after what he describes as a **minor increase in plaque formation** on coronary calcium/Cleerly CT imaging and difficulty getting LDL into the desired range through diet and supplementation alone

Evidence grade: **C — first-party disclosure of diagnoses, prescriptions and imaging interpretation**.

This materially changes how his case should be interpreted. Pascoe may be exceptionally fit and functional for his age while also having treated thyroid disease and measurable coronary atherosclerotic burden. Those facts are not contradictory: health is multidimensional, and effective medical treatment is part of health management.

It also reinforces a practical lesson: **prescribed evidence-based treatment can be more important than trying to "biohack" an abnormal risk factor indefinitely**.

## Routine and psychological flexibility

An unusually useful statement on his daily-routine page is that he does not catastrophise when life interrupts his schedule.

He says he values structure but will trade a workout, sauna session or biohack for meaningful time with other people, and then return to the routine later.

That is probably more relevant to ordinary families than many of his devices.

## Supplements and advanced interventions

Pascoe's programme also includes or has included a very large supplement protocol and advanced interventions such as:

- plasmapheresis
- hyperbaric oxygen
- stem-cell/exosome interventions
- peptides / other longevity-oriented experimentation
- sauna / cold exposure
- extensive wearable and environmental technology

He also publicly describes experimenting with a **CO2 inhalation device during exercise**.

### Project position

These advanced/experimental components **must not be interpreted as recommendations**. In particular, intentionally inhaling elevated CO2 during exercise can be hazardous and is not a normal fitness practice for unsupervised use.

The existence of these interventions also means Pascoe should not be portrayed as demonstrating that excellent health requires only cheap interventions.

## What appears genuinely impressive

### 1. Preserved function at 63

Remaining capable of running, hiking, resistance training, intervals, balance work and physically demanding events into the early 60s is meaningful regardless of epigenetic-clock scores.

### 2. Very strong grip

Self-reported grip values above 50 kg on both sides are impressive for his age and are a useful functional measurement.

### 3. Multi-domain fitness

He trains cardiovascular fitness, strength, mobility and balance instead of optimising one athletic quality.

### 4. Habit formation

His routines are designed to become automatic. That principle is highly transferable.

### 5. Social connection over perfection

His willingness to abandon an optimisation session for meaningful human connection is a valuable counterweight to rigid biohacking.

## Low-cost lessons for a working parent

1. **Train several physical qualities.** Cardio + strength + mobility + balance covers far more ground than one specialised routine.
2. **Use whole foods as the base.** Fish/seafood if desired, legumes/hummus, fruit, vegetables, nuts/seeds and minimally processed staples do not require a luxury food budget.
3. **Build habits that survive imperfect weeks.** Missing one session is not failure.
4. **Stay capable, not merely light.** Strength, grip, hiking ability, stairs and running/walking capacity matter.
5. **Keep learning, but do not copy an influencer wholesale.**
6. **Use normal healthcare first.** Blood pressure, standard bloodwork, age-appropriate screening and a GP have much higher priority than exotic tests.
7. **Protect relationships and community.** Health optimisation that consistently displaces family or connection is self-defeating.

## What a normal family can safely ignore

There is no good basis for an ordinary healthy adult to think they need:

- frequent epigenetic clocks
- quarterly biological-age competitions
- annual telomere testing
- full-body MRI screening on a schedule without a clinical indication
- very large supplement stacks
- plasmapheresis for longevity
- stem-cell/exosome treatments for anti-ageing
- specialised hypoxic/CO2 breathing devices
- a room full of recovery equipment

The low-cost core is dramatically simpler than the complete Pascoe protocol.

## Research gaps

- Verify exact DOB independently.
- Extract current measured VO2max rather than relying on qualitative claims.
- Extract latest blood pressure, resting HR, HRV, HbA1c, fasting glucose/insulin, LDL/ApoB/Lp(a), hs-CRP and DEXA values.
- Distinguish tests performed by accredited clinical labs from consumer ageing products.
- Identify any independent medical or academic verification of his current physiological claims.
- Quantify weekly training volume and sleep from current tracker data.

# Joshua Parris

## Why this profile belongs in the project

Joshua Parris is the project owner's real-world comparison case: a **32-year-old Australian working parent** with a normal household budget, full-time work/family constraints, ordinary primary-care testing, consumer wearables and non-elite exercise access.

The point of this profile is **not** to claim exceptional health.

It is to ask a more useful question:

> What can an ordinary working parent realistically learn from highly measured people, and what does that look like against a real baseline?

This profile is now a concise public summary. Joshua has explicitly asked for a much fuller longitudinal publication; the detailed record is in [the comprehensive health archive](joshua-parris-full-health-archive.md). Family members' private medical details are not included.

## Evidence quality

Most of Joshua's data are:

- **B/C** — pathology/clinic measurements personally recorded from ordinary healthcare
- **C** — consumer wearable / smart-scale measurements
- **D** — self-reported routine and exercise history

No attempt is made to convert consumer data into a diagnosis.

## What currently looks strongest

### 1. Conventional metabolic markers

Fasting glucose and HbA1c are currently within a generally favourable range, and triglycerides are low.

### 2. Resting heart rate

Sleeping RHR in the mid-to-high 50s is a reasonable baseline for a recreationally active adult.

### 3. Inflammation marker

CRP <0.4 mg/L is a low measured value at that point in time.

### 4. Kidney-function markers

Creatinine 80 µmol/L with eGFR >90 is a reassuring conventional snapshot.

### 5. Weight trend

Weight decreased from roughly **83–84 kg** in 2025/early 2026 to **77.4 kg** by July 2026.

The health meaning depends on how the change was achieved and whether lean mass was maintained; current body composition would help interpret it.

## Biggest current opportunities

Based on the public data alone, the most obvious areas to improve or monitor are:

### 1. Strength / muscle function

With cardio and daily movement already substantial, two short weekly strength sessions are now the clearest training gap.

### 2. Blood pressure trend

Repeated systolic readings in the 120s–130s are worth continuing to monitor under consistent conditions.

### 3. Repeatable aerobic fitness benchmark

A current laboratory or field VO2max/fitness benchmark is missing.

This does **not** require a lab. A repeatable 5 km time, submaximal running test, cycling test or validated wearable estimate can provide a useful trend.

### 4. Maintain current sleep improvement

Recent sleep is now close to 8 hours rather than ~6.5; preserving that matters more than pushing it higher for its own sake.

### 5. Current body composition

The last clear body-fat/muscle measurements are historical.

A consistent scale/waist trend is more useful than chasing expensive DEXA scans unless there is a clinical reason.

## What to copy from the other case studies

### From Michael Lustgarten

- keep ordinary pathology results
- compare trends over years
- walk regularly
- preserve strength
- avoid overreacting to one test

### From Julie Gibson Clark

- protect sleep
- make family-compatible activity the default
- keep meals simple rather than perfect
- use stress-management practices that fit normal life

### From Oskar Svendsen

- most aerobic work does not need to be hard
- compare fitness with your own baseline
- consistency matters more than elite numbers

### From Scott Kelly

- schedule physical maintenance during stressful periods
- expect recovery to take time
- resilience is not the same as never struggling

### From Bryan Johnson

- measure enough to know whether basics are working
- treat persistent abnormal findings seriously
- do not assume expensive interventions are responsible for every good number

### From Bob Troia

- track only measurements that can change a decision
- repeat experiments before believing a result

## Low-cost priority order

For Joshua's public comparison case, the most useful next data are deliberately ordinary:

1. **home blood-pressure trend**
2. **current waist**
3. **repeatable 5 km benchmark**
4. **repeatable strength benchmark**
5. **monthly same-source RHR/HRV/sleep/steps summaries**
6. **annual / clinically appropriate standard pathology**

Not currently necessary for this project:

- repeated epigenetic clocks
- telomere testing
- broad microbiome testing
- full-body MRI
- multi-omics
- huge supplement stacks

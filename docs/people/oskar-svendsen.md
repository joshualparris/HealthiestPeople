# Oskar Nikolai Birger Svendsen

## Why he is in the comparison

Oskar Svendsen is one of the strongest documented examples of extreme human aerobic physiology. Unlike many longevity-influencer metrics, his key measurements were collected in a laboratory and published in the peer-reviewed *Journal of Applied Physiology*.

His case is deliberately narrower than Johnson, Lustgarten, Pascoe or Kelly: we have unusually strong evidence about aerobic performance, but much less public information about his current metabolic, cardiovascular, sleep or imaging profile.

## Background

- Full name: **Oskar Nikolai Birger Svendsen**
- Date of birth: **10 April 1994**
- Birthplace: Lillehammer, Norway
- Former professional cyclist
- Junior World Champion, individual time trial, 2012
- Team Joker professional/continental rider, 2013–2014
- Retired from elite cycling at about age 20

The UCI confirms his DOB and competition history.

## Laboratory VO2max trajectory

The 2019 peer-reviewed case study documented his progression from alpine skier to elite cyclist.

| Date / period | VO2max (mL/kg/min) | Context |
|---|---:|---|
| Mar 2010 | **74.6** | before bike-specific endurance development |
| Apr 2010 | **83.4** | early training |
| Feb 2011 | **86.8** | training |
| Sep 2011 | **85.1** | training |
| Aug 2012 | **96.7** | peak peer-reviewed value |
| Dec 2012 | **92.8** | same championship year |
| Nov 2013 | **86.5** | professional development period |
| Oct 2015 | **77.0** | after ~15 months with almost no structured exercise |

At the peak test:

- body mass: **76.5 kg**
- absolute VO2max: **7,397 mL/min**
- relative VO2max: **96.7 mL/kg/min**
- improvement from his initial 74.6 result: **29.6%**

Even after substantial detraining, his 77.0 mL/kg/min result remained extremely high.

## Lactate-threshold / performance development

The same paper reported an improvement of up to **20.9% in power output at a blood lactate concentration of 4 mmol/L**.

That matters because VO2max is not the whole of endurance performance. Sustainable fraction of VO2max, exercise economy, threshold power, skill, psychology and race craft also influence results.

## Training exposure

The case study includes his monthly training volume and distribution among low-, moderate- and high-intensity zones.

The researchers' central finding was that Svendsen began with an unusually high aerobic capacity even before specialising in cycling and then responded strongly to several years of dedicated endurance training.

### Doping controls

From autumn 2012 through summer 2014 he underwent **eight anti-doping controls**, all reported negative in the peer-reviewed case study.

His haematocrit over those tests ranged from **45.1% to 48.8%**, mean **47.0%**.

## Measurement controversy: 96.7 vs 97.5

Popular media often quote **97.5 mL/kg/min**.

For this repository, the primary scientific value should be **96.7 mL/kg/min**, because that is the value published in the peer-reviewed longitudinal case report.

The paper's authors explicitly addressed whether the result might reflect measurement error and reported that the equipment had been properly calibrated.

## Current life

A 2026 *Cycling Weekly* interview located Svendsen roughly 12 years after leaving elite cycling.

He reported that he:

- lives in Oslo
- works in business/operations rather than professional sport
- remains physically active
- runs, skis, cycles and does other sports
- does not regret leaving elite cycling

A contemporary work profile lists him in strategy/operations at Auk.

### 2025/26 VO2max retest

The 2026 feature reported that he returned to a laboratory for another VO2max test as part of a stag-do joke.

The current value was **not publicly disclosed** in the reporting available to this project.

That is important: we should not pretend his teenage 96.7 result describes his current physiology at age 32.

## What appears genuinely impressive

### 1. Exceptional intrinsic aerobic capacity

His pre-specialisation value of **74.6** was already at the level expected of highly trained endurance athletes.

This strongly suggests favourable innate physiology, although "genetics" should not be reduced to a single known gene.

### 2. Large training response

Three years of dedicated cycling raised the measured VO2max from 74.6 to 96.7 — an extraordinary combination of high baseline and training responsiveness.

### 3. Robust retained capacity

After ~15 months with little structured training he still measured **77.0**, showing that much of his unusually large aerobic engine remained.

### 4. A useful antidote to optimisation obsession

Svendsen voluntarily left professional cycling. His story is a reminder that maximum physiological performance is not identical to a good life, mental wellbeing, meaningful work or sustainable health behaviour.

## What we *do not* know

Public evidence is currently inadequate for a whole-health comparison on:

- LDL/ApoB/Lp(a)
- blood pressure
- HbA1c / fasting glucose / insulin
- body fat / visceral fat
- coronary calcium
- kidney/liver markers
- sleep duration/quality
- HRV
- bone density
- current resting heart rate
- current VO2max
- major current diagnoses

Therefore he should score extremely highly for **documented aerobic physiology**, but not automatically rank above people with broader health datasets.

## Low-cost lessons for an ordinary working parent

1. **Aerobic capacity matters, but you do not need elite numbers.** The useful goal is improving your own fitness from baseline.
2. **Consistency over years beats a short heroic block.**
3. **Most endurance training can be easier work.** Elite endurance programmes include large amounts of low-intensity volume, not constant maximal sessions.
4. **Use occasional hard efforts.** Higher-intensity work can complement easier aerobic activity when health and injury status allow.
5. **Walking, running, cycling and hills are enough.** VO2max improvement does not require a premium gym.
6. **Fitness is partly inherited. Compare yourself with yourself.** Svendsen's starting point was already extraordinary.
7. **Do not equate athletic performance with whole-person health.**
8. **A sustainable life can matter more than maximising a metric.** He chose to leave elite sport despite unusual physical potential.

## Practical family version

A normal adult could capture most of the relevant principle with:

- brisk walking most days
- two to three short aerobic sessions weekly
- one harder interval-style session when appropriately conditioned
- active family outings
- cycling/running for transport or recreation
- gradual progression rather than trying to mimic professional training hours

No laboratory VO2max test is required to benefit. A repeatable field test, running/cycling pace, hill performance or wearable estimate can be used as a trend measure while recognising its limitations.

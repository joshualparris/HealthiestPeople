How to read this site

Evidence & interpretation

Interesting people produce interesting data. That does not automatically turn self-tracking into proof, and it does not make measurement density the same thing as health.

GradesRulesResearch docs

Evidence ladder

Interesting is not the same as proven.

The site separates peer-reviewed measurements, independently reported clinical results, first-party data and self-report. Missing data stays missing rather than becoming an invented score.

AIndependent peer-reviewed or institutional measurement
BIndependently reported clinical or laboratory result
CFirst-party published measurement or protocol
DSelf-report or interview

Interpretation rules

Five guardrails

1

No invented scores

If different people were measured differently, the site does not force them into one fake leaderboard.

2

Context travels with the number

Age, date, method, medication, training state and source quality can materially change interpretation.

3

Association is not causation

A favourable result does not prove every part of that person's routine caused it.

4

Transferability matters

A useful practice must survive work, kids, cost, time and ordinary life.

5

Clinical signals go to clinicians

Persistent abnormalities deserve appropriate medical follow-up rather than an ever-larger self-testing stack.

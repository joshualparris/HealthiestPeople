What the audit checks

1

Dates and context

Historical peaks, current measurements and single observations should not be presented as though they are the same thing.

2

Source strength

Peer-reviewed or institutional evidence is kept separate from independently reported results, first-party data and interviews.

3

Guideline vs project choice

Population health guidance is labelled separately from HealthiestPeople's own practical fallback rules or planning choices.

4

Units and methods

Values should keep their measurement method, unit and device/lab context where those details affect interpretation.

5

No invented certainty

Missing values stay missing; summaries and percentiles are not converted into fake raw numbers.

6

Internal links

Automated CI now checks local pages, assets and rendered research-document routes for broken links.

Limits

What this audit cannot guarantee

HealthiestPeople combines peer-reviewed research, public records, first-party self-tracking and personal datasets. Sources can change, public pages can disappear, and some values remain self-reported or device-dependent. The site should therefore be read as an evidence-aware research project, not a clinical record or a substitute for professional care.

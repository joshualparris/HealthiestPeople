What high-longevity countries can actually teach us

Japan, Spain, Switzerland, Iceland, Singapore, South Korea and Australia all have strong health outcomes in some domains — and obvious weaknesses in others. This page extracts only the lessons that survive official statistics and peer-reviewed research.

Countries Shared lessons Josh Sources

Important context

There is no scientifically agreed “healthiest country”.

Life expectancy, healthy life expectancy, preventable mortality, treatable mortality, obesity, smoking, activity, mental health and inequality can tell different stories. The useful question is which behaviours and systems repeatedly show up alongside strong outcomes.

Best use of country comparisons Borrow evidence-backed strengths. Do not copy a country wholesale.

Country-by-country

Strengths, caveats and transferable lessons

Recent official metrics are paired with peer-reviewed evidence where the country has a particularly useful lesson.

Japan

Copy the food pattern, not the salt or inactivity

84.1-year life expectancy in OECD 2025 data, with low preventable mortality. Japanese cohort/meta-analysis evidence supports dietary patterns rich in vegetables, fruit, fish, soy, fibre and minimally processed foods. But sodium is a recognised weakness, and OECD data show substantial inactivity and smoking.

Practical versionMore fish/soy/veg/fruit/whole foods; keep salt sensible.
OECD Japan 2025 ↗ Japanese diet meta-analysis ↗ Reduced-salt Japanese diet ↗
Spain

The strongest country-specific diet trial

Spain reports 84.0-year life expectancy. The PREDIMED randomised trial provides unusually strong evidence for a Mediterranean dietary pattern using vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts, olive oil and useful protein. Spain's higher smoking and alcohol levels are reasons not to copy everything.

Practical versionMediterranean food pattern, without treating wine as a health intervention.
OECD Spain 2025 ↗ Corrected PREDIMED trial ↗ Mediterranean diet evidence review ↗
Switzerland

Healthy behaviour + good treatment both matter

OECD 2025 reports 84.3-year life expectancy, low obesity, relatively good activity and exceptionally low treatable mortality of 34 per 100,000. That is a reminder that healthcare quality belongs in a longevity model.

Practical versionUse ordinary preventive care well; don't try to biohack around a treatable risk factor.
OECD Switzerland 2025 ↗
Iceland

Population risk factors can move mortality enormously

A peer-reviewed analysis found coronary-heart-disease mortality fell about 80% from 1981–2006; modelling attributed about 73% of the decline to risk-factor improvements, especially cholesterol, smoking and blood pressure. Modern Iceland also has very low smoking.

Practical versionBP, smoking and lipids are not boring side notes — they are high-impact levers.
Iceland CHD analysis ↗ OECD Iceland 2025 ↗
Singapore

Stack ordinary healthy behaviours and organise prevention

Singapore resident life expectancy was 83.5 years in 2024. Its Healthier SG programme explicitly links people with a family doctor, screening, vaccination and a personalised health plan. A Singapore Chinese Health Study analysis found markedly lower mortality among people combining multiple healthy lifestyle factors.

Practical versionOne boring preventive-care system + several ordinary habits beats one magic biomarker.
Singapore MOH statistics ↗ Healthier SG ↗ Singapore lifestyle cohort ↗
South Korea

Low obesity is not the same as complete health

OECD 2025 reports 83.5-year life expectancy and very low self-reported obesity, but also 61% insufficient physical activity and a high suicide rate. Korean sodium research also highlights cardiovascular trade-offs in traditional diets.

Practical versionNever turn BMI or body weight into the whole health score.
OECD Korea 2025 ↗ Korean sodium evidence ↗
Australia

Keep the strengths; fix the weak spots

Australia already performs well: 83.0-year life expectancy, low daily smoking and low preventable/treatable mortality. The obvious population weaknesses are higher obesity and alcohol use. You do not need to reject Australian guidance to learn from Japan or Spain.

Practical versionKeep evidence-based Australian prevention; borrow only the specific food/activity lessons that add value.
OECD Australia 2025 ↗

What survives across countries

The repeatable signal

These lessons are backed by broader evidence beyond the country examples.

Don't smokeStrong

Low smoking and smoking reduction repeatedly track with better population outcomes.

Know BP + lipidsStrong

Iceland shows how major risk-factor shifts can change cardiovascular mortality.

Plant-rich whole-food patternStrong

Japanese and Mediterranean evidence converge on vegetables, legumes/soy, fruit, whole grains, nuts and useful protein.

Move oftenStrong

Large prospective meta-analyses support mortality benefit without elite exercise volumes.

Preserve strengthStrong observational support

Strengthening activity adds a health dimension that national life-expectancy statistics cannot show directly.

Use healthcareStrong practical logic

Low treatable mortality is a reminder that prevention and effective treatment complement lifestyle.

Keep salt sensibleStrong

Japanese/Korean evidence makes clear that a traditional diet can still contain a harmful component.

Keep UPFs secondaryModerate evidence

Multiple umbrella reviews associate higher ultra-processed-food exposure with worse cardiometabolic and mortality outcomes.

Protect social connectionStrong observational support

Large meta-analysis associates social isolation/loneliness with higher mortality.

What this changes for Josh

Very little extra time. Mostly better defaults.

Your current activity is already high. The country evidence strengthens the existing plan rather than adding hours of exercise or expensive testing.

Keep Monday Frisbee + Saturday parkrun.

You already have vigorous/team activity and family aerobic work. Country evidence gives no reason to chase more daily steps.

Keep Tuesday/Thursday strength.

Strength remains the clearest training gap and takes only about 40 extra minutes/week.

Use Japan + Spain for food.

Vegetables, fruit, legumes/soy, whole grains, nuts/seeds, fish/useful protein and olive oil. No need for wine or salty imported longevity foods.

Make prevention boringly reliable.

Home BP baseline, clinically appropriate lipids/glucose/iron follow-up, vaccination and age/risk-appropriate screening.

Protect social activity.

Frisbee, family movement, church/community and relationships fit the wider evidence; they are not obstacles to optimisation.

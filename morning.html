Josh's weekday morning

Healthy without becoming a 5 am biohacker.

Hard constraint: leave home at 8:15 am. The routine below protects sleep, includes Sylvie and Elias, gives you morning outdoor light and a decent breakfast, and adds almost no “health admin”.

6:20

Wake

Open curtains, bathroom, short prayer/gratitude. Keep work/research/social feeds closed for now.

6:25

Breakfast prep + kids

Start breakfast and help Sylvie/Elias get moving. A normal glass of water is enough; no “detox” drink.

6:35

Family breakfast

Josh default: 2 eggs + 2 slices wholemeal toast + 1 piece of fruit + 20 g nuts. Sit with the kids when practical.

6:55

15 minutes outdoors

Backyard, short walk, bins or simple play with Sylvie and Elias. This is for daylight and family movement, not a workout.

7:10

Kids ready

Clothes, teeth, hair and bags. Help whichever child needs you rather than trying to run a perfect routine.

7:30

Shower

Normal comfortable shower. No cold-shower requirement.

7:40

Dress + groom

Teeth, deodorant, clothes. Sunscreen when today's UV/outdoor exposure calls for it.

7:50

Final family block

Shoes, bags, any last help for the kids. Keep a little connection/buffer rather than filling the slot with another task.

8:05

Departure buffer

Toilet, keys, phone, wallet, water bottle. Leave at 8:15 sharp.

What the research supports

High-value, low-hype

Sleep

Don't wake earlier just to optimise.

Australian guidance recommends 7–9 hours with consistent sleep/wake times. Your recent sleep is already around 8 hours, so protect it.

Australian Government ↗Sleep Health Foundation ↗
Morning light

Go outside early; don't stare at the sun.

Light is a circadian timing cue. Evidence supports early-day outdoor light as a sensible sleep/circadian habit, though it is not a proven longevity hack.

2021 systematic review ↗2019 meta-analysis ↗2025 sunlight study ↗
Breakfast

No magical minute after waking.

Trials do not establish that breakfast is universally necessary for weight control. For you it earns its place because it is nutritious, family-compatible and helps spread protein through the day.

2020 RCT meta-analysis ↗2023 meta-analysis ↗Australian Dietary Guidelines ↗
Family

Breakfast can do double duty.

Family-meal research is mostly observational, but consistently links more family meals with better diet quality and family functioning. It does not need to be an elaborate meal.

2023 umbrella review ↗2020 systematic review ↗2019 meta-analysis ↗
Sun safety

Daylight ≠ chase UV.

In Australia, use SunSmart protection when UV is 3 or above. Morning light exposure is about the light signal, not tanning.

Cancer Council UV Index ↗SunSmart guidance ↗
Protein

Spread it; don't save it all for dinner.

Controlled feeding research suggests a more even protein distribution can support muscle protein synthesis. The exact perfect breakfast dose is less certain, so the goal is simply a meaningful protein contribution.

Controlled feeding study ↗2024 scoping review ↗

Bottom line

Wake 6:20 → breakfast with the kids → outside 15 min → get everyone ready → shower → leave 8:15.

The “optimal” part is not adding more. It is keeping the highest-value behaviours compatible with the family you actually have.

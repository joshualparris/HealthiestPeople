          What survives across the case studies

          The ordinary-person stack

          1

          Move most days

          Walking counts. So do family outings, cycling, running, chores and active transport.

          2

          Strength twice a week

          Squat, hinge, push, pull and carry. Preserve useful capacity, not gym status.

          3

          Build aerobic fitness

          Easy volume plus some harder work. Improve your own baseline rather than chasing elite numbers.

          4

          Protect sleep

          Australian guidance now recommends 7–9 hours with consistent bed and wake times for adults.

          5

          Eat mostly ordinary food

          Vegetables, fruit, wholegrains, legumes, nuts, seeds and practical protein beat branded longevity food.

          6

          Measure with a question

          Blood pressure, routine pathology and a few trends can be useful. More data is not automatically better.

          7

          Follow persistent signals

          A repeated abnormality deserves sensible clinical follow-up rather than endless self-experimentation.

          8

          Keep relationships inside the plan

          A health protocol that regularly costs family connection, rest or peace is badly designed.

          Australian baseline: adults should be active most days, do muscle-strengthening on 2+ days each week, break up long sitting, and aim for 7–9 hours of good-quality sleep with consistent timing. Department of Health ↗

          Evidence rules

          Interesting is not the same as proven.

          The site deliberately separates peer-reviewed measurements, clinical reports, first-party data and self-report. Missing data stays missing rather than becoming an invented score.

          AIndependent peer-reviewed or institutional measurement
          BIndependently reported clinical or laboratory result
          CFirst-party published measurement or protocol
          DSelf-report or interview

          A useful stopping rule

          Before buying another health thing, ask:

          1. What decision will this measurement change?
          2. Have I already covered sleep, movement, strength, food and basic preventive care?
          3. Will this make family life easier, neutral, or harder?
          4. Can I sustain it for a year without turning health into a second job?
